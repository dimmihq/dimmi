# How the dimmi site talks

The site's voice is modelled on how PostHog writes: like a smart friend who
happens to sell software, not a vendor trying to close you. Every page
follows this guide. Borrow the attitude, never their sentences.

## The idea

- Say the true thing plainly, then stop. Clear beats clever, every time.
- Write to one person. Use "you" and "we". Use contractions. Active voice, present tense.
- Take a position. No "it depends", no hedging, no hype.
- Be on the reader's side about money. Tell them how to pay us less. Tell them when we are the wrong pick.
- Jokes are welcome only when they are specific, dry and true. They live in
  small places: a section title, a caption, a footnote, a FAQ answer's first
  word. A joke never replaces the information; the fact comes right after it.
- At most one joke per section. A page that winks in every line is exhausting.

## Devices we use

- **Headlines say what it does or what you get.** "One inbox for every channel", not "Reimagine support".
- **Section titles can be the reader's blunt question** or a dry aside: "How to give us even less money", "Then why would I pay?", "What counts as a conversation?".
- **FAQ questions are what a sceptical buyer would actually type.** Answers open with the verdict ("Yes.", "No.", "Nope.", "Mostly.") and then explain in one to three sentences.
- **Prices are stated as facts, free first.** Numbers on the page, before any pitch.
- **Honesty about limits.** Each product page has one FAQ entry that names who should not use it: "Can I run a call center on it?", "Is this a BI tool?". The limit is a position we hold (dimmi is not a call center, a BI tool or Zapier), not a gap a competitor on the pricing page fills.
- **The site describes the product we are shipping.** Planned features that match Intercom, Zendesk, Help Scout, Front and Freshdesk are written as features. Never concede one of them in a FAQ.
- **Competitors are named and treated fairly.** Concede what they do well.
- **No-sales stance.** You can use everything without talking to us. "Talk to a human" is optional and we say so.
- **CTAs are literal and carry the price.** "Start free", "Self-host it", "Start free, no card".
- **Features are a concrete action plus its outcome.** "Snooze a conversation and it comes back when the customer replies", not "powerful snoozing".

## Mechanics

- "dimmi" is always lowercase, even at the start of a sentence.
- Sentence case for every heading and button.
- American spelling. Oxford comma.
- No em dashes. Use a full stop or a comma instead.
- Straight quotes. No emoji. No exclamation marks, except inside an obvious parody.
- Spell out zero to nine, numerals from 10 up and for prices, percentages and limits.
- Short sentences. Short paragraphs. If a sentence needs a second comma, split it.

## Never write

leverage, utilize, streamline, robust, seamless, best-in-class, holistic,
synergy, unlock, empower, supercharge, revolutionize, game-changing,
cutting-edge, next-generation, world-class, "helps you to", "allows you to"
(say what it does), "AI-powered" as a selling point on its own.

## Never do

- Invent a feature, a number, a customer or a guarantee to land a line.
- Change a fact to make a joke work. Prices, limits and what a feature does stay exactly as they are.
- Attack a competitor unfairly, or make fun of customers.
- Mention our costs or margins.
- Copy a PostHog line. The voice is the lesson, the words are ours.

## A quick test

Read the line out loud to a friend who runs a support team. If you would
feel silly saying it, rewrite it. If they would say "so what does it do?",
you have not said it yet.
