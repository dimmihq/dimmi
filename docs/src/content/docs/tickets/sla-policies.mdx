---
title: "Set SLA policies"
description: "Set first reply, next reply, follow-up and resolution targets, choose what they apply to and what happens on a breach."
---

Create SLA policies in **Settings → SLA policies**. [How SLA timers work](/tickets/how-sla-timers-work/) explains when each target's clock starts, pauses and stops.

## Targets and clock

- A policy needs at least one target. Each target is 1 minute to 1 year.
- **Warn before a breach:** 15 minutes by default, up to 7 days.
- **Office hours:** the [office hours](/inbox/office-hours/) schedule the clocks count in. A new policy uses the default schedule. **Around the clock (24/7)** counts all time.
- **Pause while waiting on the customer:** on by default.
- **Pause while waiting on another team:** off by default.

A change to a target applies to clocks that start after you save.

## What a policy applies to

| Work | Gets a policy when |
| --- | --- |
| A new ticket | Its type and its priority match the policy's **Applies to** lists. An empty list matches any. |
| A conversation | A workflow runs the **Apply an SLA** step. If the conversation has a customer ticket, the ticket gets the policy too. |

- When several policies match a new ticket, the one created first applies.
- A policy with both lists empty applies only from workflows.
- dimmi matches a ticket once, when the ticket is created.

## On breach

- **Raise priority to** never lowers a priority that is already higher.
- **Move to team** changes the team and keeps the assignee.

To do more, start a workflow from the **SLA about to breach** or **SLA breached** trigger. See [triggers and steps](/workflows/triggers-and-steps/).

## Archive

Archiving a policy cancels every clock it is running, and you cannot undo it.
