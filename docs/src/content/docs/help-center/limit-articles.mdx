---
title: "Limit articles to some customers"
description: "Keep an article to one audience, and the three ways a reader signs in to your help center to see it."
---

To keep an article to some customers, open **Article settings** and set **Audience** to one of your saved [audiences](/customers/audiences/).

## What an audience limit does

- **Readers:** the article leaves the help center's pages and search for everyone outside the audience.
- **dimmi agent:** the agent never answers a customer from an article that customer could not read.
- **Timing:** the change saves at once, without publishing the article.
- **Collections:** to keep a whole collection to an audience, call `PATCH /api/v1/collections/{collection}/audience`.

dimmi refuses an audience that uses page conditions or any value a browser could claim. An audience that decides access may use only what your own systems told dimmi about the customer.

## How readers sign in

The help center shows **Sign in** when an audience hides something from the reader, or when you set a login address. Set both options under **Readers sign in with** in **Settings → Help centers**.

| Way in | How it works |
| --- | --- |
| **Email code** | On by default. The reader types their email and a 6-digit code. The code works for 10 minutes and 5 wrong tries cancel it. |
| **Your product's login** | The reader signs in to your product, which sends them back with an identity token. |
| Messenger | A customer the messenger already knows opens an article from the messenger signed in. |

- **Email code limits:** one code every 30 seconds and 5 an hour for each address.
- **Who the reader is:** a code signs the reader in as the customer with that email address, or as a new lead.
- **Session:** a reader stays signed in for 7 days after their last visit, on that help center only.

## Sign readers in through your product

1. Set **Your product's login** to an `https` page of your product, such as `https://app.acme.example/help-login`.
2. dimmi sends the reader to that page with a `return_to` query parameter.
3. Your page signs the user in, then signs an [identity token](/developers/messenger/identity-verification/) for them on your server.
4. Your page redirects to `return_to` with `#token=<identity_token>` added.

```text
https://acme.dimmi.help/sso?next=%2Farticles%2Fbilling#token=<identity_token>
```

Redirect only when `return_to` starts with your help center's address.

A token with an `aud` claim must name `dimmi`, `dimmi-help` or the workspace ID. A token with a `nonce` claim works once.
