---
title: "How the inbox works"
description: "How conversations arrive in dimmi, the views that list them, and how statuses, snoozing and reopening work."
---

A conversation lands in the inbox its channel belongs to and is open, snoozed or closed. Spam and the trash are flags beside the status.

## Views

Below the fixed views, dimmi lists your [saved views](/inbox/views/), then a view for each ticket type, team and teammate.

- **Unassigned:** hidden from teammates whose access leaves out conversations nobody holds.
- **Snoozed:** only the snoozed conversations you hold.
- **Deleted:** the trash, shown only to roles that can delete conversations.
- **Inboxes:** a view for each inbox, once the workspace has two.

## Closing and reopening

A customer's reply within the inbox's **Reopen window** reopens a closed conversation. A later reply starts a new conversation. The window is 30 days in a new inbox.

Automatic mail, such as an out-of-office reply, never reopens or wakes a conversation.

## Snoozing

Snooze presets use your time zone, or the workspace's when yours is not set.

| Preset | Wakes |
| --- | --- |
| **Later today** | In 3 hours, rounded up to the quarter hour. |
| **Tomorrow** | Tomorrow at 09:00. |
| **Next week** | Next Monday at 09:00. |
| **In one week** | In 7 days, at 09:00. |

- A snooze lasts at least 1 minute and at most 1 year.
- A customer's message wakes a snoozed conversation. By default, so does a teammate's reply.
- A closed conversation cannot be snoozed. Reopen it first.

## Who holds a conversation

The first teammate to reply to an unassigned conversation takes it, unless the inbox turns off **Assign to whoever replies**. To give new conversations to teammates without a reply, [set an assignment mode](/inbox/inboxes-and-assignment/).

## Spam and the trash

- **Spam:** after you mark a conversation as spam, new conversations from the same customer start in **Spam**. So do conversations from a blocked customer.
- **Deleted:** a deleted conversation leaves every list and count but keeps its messages, so you can restore it.
