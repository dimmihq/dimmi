**dimmi Cloud** and **self-hosted** dimmi run the exact same open source code under MIT. Neither edition holds features back behind an enterprise paywall.

The differences come down to who runs the server and who supplies outside services like email and AI keys.

## Side-by-side comparison

| Area | dimmi Cloud | Self-hosted |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Who runs it** | dimmi hosts it at `trydimmi.com` | You run it on [one Linux server](/self-hosting/install/) |
| **Sign-up** | Open to anyone | Invite-only (your first owner invites the team) |
| **Forwarded email** | Ready-to-use addresses on `inbound.trydimmi.com` | Your own inbound email domain |
| **Help center address** | A subdomain on `dimmi.help` (plus custom domains) | `/hc/<subdomain>` on your dimmi host (plus custom domains) |
| **Team invitations** | Up to 100 per workspace each day | Unlimited |
| **Audit log history** | Kept for **365 days** | Kept forever |
| **Bulk email protection** | Pauses a workspace's broadcasts, series, and rating emails past a **5%** bounce rate or **0.1%** spam complaint rate | Off until you [set the rates](/self-hosting/configuration-options/) |
| **Messenger branding** | Shows a dimmi badge until your workspace pays an invoice | No badge unless you turn it on |
| **Cost** | **100 free conversations** each month, unlimited seats, usage pricing after | **$0** to dimmi (you pay your server host) |

## Outside services when self-hosting

On dimmi Cloud, email forwarding, Slack, and included AI models work out of the box.

When you self-host, you connect your own credentials for [email](/self-hosting/email/), [AI providers](/self-hosting/ai-providers/), and [integrations](/self-hosting/integrations/) like Slack or Google sign-in.
